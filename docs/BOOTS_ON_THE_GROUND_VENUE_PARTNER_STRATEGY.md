# Pin2Win: boots-on-the-ground venue partner strategy

Field playbook for the partner leading local golf-simulator venue outreach. Prepared September 15, 2026. San Antonio and nearby Boerne are the first territory because Pin2Win's current listed venue is Alamo Golf Den. Expand the radius only after the first repeatable venue pilot.

## The objective

Find independently operated simulator venues willing to test whether a Pin2Win skill challenge adds a reason to book a bay, gives existing customers another experience to share, and creates a measurable venue-specific funnel. The first ask is a **20-minute owner conversation and a no-payment walkthrough**, not a contract or a public launch.

The acquisition target for the first 30 days is a working list of 20 qualified venues, 10 owner/GM conversations, 3 onsite walkthroughs, and 1–2 written pilot proposals. These are planning targets, not demand forecasts. Review the conversion rates weekly and adjust the territory or pitch before adding more visits.

## Positioning: sell the venue outcome, not the prize

Pin2Win is an optional, separately paid golf skill challenge layered onto a venue's normal simulator booking. A customer books bay time **with the venue**, scans an authorized bay QR code onsite, signs in or creates an account, pays Pin2Win through Square, receives challenge access, and plays under published rules. Pin2Win handles its own account, payment, challenge, and prize administration; the venue controls its own bookings, admission, staff, and facility. The initial challenge entry fee currently published in the rules is $20, separate from bay rental. Never imply that this fee goes to the venue or that the venue sponsors the prize without a signed agreement.

Owner-facing value propositions to test, rather than claim as proven:

- An additional reason for customers to book a session or return with friends.
- A short event format for leagues, member nights, or corporate groups without replacing the venue's booking system.
- A location-specific booking link, bay QR flow, and reporting that can show activity from that venue.
- Pin2Win-provided signage, customer copy, staff training, and issue escalation so the owner can judge the workload before committing.

Lead with the owner's goals. For a membership club, ask about member engagement; for a social lounge, ask about group nights and quieter booking periods; for a coaching studio, ask whether a challenge fits its brand at all. A performance-only facility may be a poor fit, and that is useful to learn early.

## Build the target list before driving

Use Google Maps/Search and venue websites to find indoor golf venues; Google's [Business Profile guidance](https://business.google.com/us/business-profile/) explains why Maps and Search are useful for local business discovery. Cross-check simulator technology and bookings on the venue's own site or by calling the venue. The [Trackman locator](https://www.trackman.com/locator) is another discovery tool, **not** proof that Pin2Win can run on a particular bay.

For each prospect, record: venue name/address, owner or GM, direct contact method, bay count, simulator software and version, booking model, leagues/events, peak and slow periods, current challenge/tournament offers, suitability for an onsite QR, likely customer fit, and the source/date verified. Do not treat a lead as technically qualified until an operator confirms how an eligible shot and timestamp can be captured and reviewed.

### Priority score (0–10)

Score 0, 1, or 2 on each dimension: (1) owner access, (2) customer/event fit, (3) ability to dedicate a bay and staff for a test, (4) plausible simulator evidence path, and (5) interest in promoting through its own channels. Visit 8–10 leads first, including different venue models; leave low-scoring leads for later. A high score earns a walkthrough, **not** permission to launch.

## The field sequence

1. **Prepare a one-page leave-behind and a phone demo.** Show the venue listing, booking handoff, onsite QR-to-Square path, and an example admin report. Bring a proposed test-night outline, not a prize poster. Use a test account and sandbox/demo checkout; never take a live customer payment to demonstrate an unapproved venue.
2. **Make a short introduction.** Call or email to learn when the owner/GM is present. Walk in at a quiet time, buy a normal session or ask for permission to see a bay, and avoid interrupting staff or customers. If the owner is absent, leave a one-page note and ask for a specific callback time.
3. **Run a discovery conversation.** Ask about bookings, repeat visits, leagues, current promos, bay software, staffing, and what would make a pilot worth the effort. Spend more time listening than presenting.
4. **Offer an onsite walkthrough.** With the operator, trace one player from booking to QR scan to payment to simulator setup to recorded result and support escalation. Capture missing steps. If verification is not possible, propose only a technical dry run, not a paid pilot.
5. **Send a tailored pilot proposal within 24 hours.** State what the venue said it wants, the chosen bay/time, operator duties, marketing channels, success measure, support path, and the remaining prerequisites. Ask for a yes/no decision date.
6. **Follow up once or twice, then close the loop.** Suggested cadence: next-day recap, day-4 question, day-10 final check. If there is no interest, record the objection and request permission to reconnect after a relevant product milestone. Do not repeatedly drop in uninvited.

### 30-second opener

> “Hi, I'm [name] with Pin2Win here in San Antonio. We work with simulator venues on optional, QR-based golf skill challenges that can give customers another reason to book a bay. Your booking stays yours. I'm not asking you to launch anything today—could I ask how you currently run leagues or promotions and see whether a small test would make sense here?”

### Owner discovery questions

- What are you trying to improve this quarter: new bay bookings, repeat visits, member engagement, or group events?
- Which days or times would you actually want to fill? Which times must a test avoid?
- Do you currently run closest-to-pin, hole-in-one, league, or corporate events? What works and what creates work for staff?
- What simulator hardware/software/version is in each candidate bay? Can it export a session, shot ID, exact shot time, holed flag, and an audit trail?
- Who would set up and supervise a challenge? What is a realistic training and support burden?
- How do guests book, and may we link directly to your booking page? Would you promote a test via a member email, event calendar, or in-venue signage?
- What would have to be true after a 30-day pilot for you to keep it?

### Follow-up email

> Subject: A small Pin2Win pilot for [venue]
>
> Thanks for walking me through [specific venue goal]. A possible test is one bay on [day/time], with customers continuing to book through [venue booking path]. Pin2Win would provide the venue-specific QR and customer instructions; your operator would handle [agreed simulator step]. Before any paid launch, we would confirm result evidence, onsite presence, rules, support, and the commercial terms in writing. Would a 20-minute bay walkthrough on [two specific options] be useful?

### Common objections

| Objection | Response |
| --- | --- |
| “We already run tournaments.” | “Good. This should complement, not replace, yours. Which part of your current event has the most friction?” |
| “Staff cannot manage another system.” | “Let's map the exact operator steps first. If it takes too much staff time, we will not propose a paid pilot.” |
| “Will this cannibalize our booking revenue?” | “Guests keep booking and paying for bay time through you. Pin2Win's entry is separate. We can measure whether bookings or return visits change, not assume an uplift.” |
| “Does it work on our simulator?” | “We cannot promise that from the brand name. We need a bay-level setup and result-evidence walkthrough.” |
| “Who handles a disputed payment or winner?” | “Pin2Win handles its payment, entry, refund, and prize review. The pilot agreement will spell out what venue records or operator observations may be needed.” |

## Pilot offer and commercial boundaries

Propose a **non-exclusive, one-venue/one-bay, 30-day pilot** only after technical and compliance readiness. Suggested structure for negotiation, not a preapproved contract:

- Venue retains control of bay pricing, reservations, admission, and customer service for venue charges. Pin2Win retains control of its challenge fee, payment, rules, refunds, and prize administration.
- Pin2Win supplies QR signage, booking-link placement, landing-page copy, a staff quick-reference card, test accounts, weekly activity reporting, and a named support contact.
- Venue supplies an approved bay/time, software/operator access, accurate booking link, a staff contact, and agreed result/incident records. No extra hardware or vendor integration is promised.
- Marketing is opt-in and coordinated: venue approves use of its name/logo and signs before publication; each party owns its own customer list. Do not collect or use the venue's customer contacts without an agreed permission path.
- Commission, revenue share, exclusivity, sponsorship, and ad spend are **not preset**. Document any such term in a separate written venue agreement reviewed with counsel/accounting. Do not quote a percentage in an outreach meeting.
- Both parties get a written pause/stop procedure for payment outages, missing result evidence, unsafe setup, or disputes.

The most credible first pilot is a supervised event at an off-peak time. A 24/7 or unattended venue may require a different onsite and evidence control; do not force the same model onto it.

### Launch gate: do not skip this

The [platform compliance checklist](PLATFORM_COMPLIANCE_IMPLEMENTATION_CHECKLIST.md) still identifies transaction-specific onsite verification and real simulator-result integration as open. Before a **new paid venue launch**, Pin2Win operations must sign off on: current Challenge Details and published rules for that venue/bay; permitted Texas/18+ audience; prize funding/insurance and any venue-specific requirements; a reliable onsite-presence control; a tested Square payment-and-return flow; an authenticated simulator evidence path and winner review; refunds/support/reconciliation; and a written venue agreement. If these are not ready, continue acquisition conversations and technical dry runs only. Never make “guaranteed winner,” numerical odds, vendor endorsement, or “live at your venue” claims prematurely.

## Measure the work and decide what to do next

Maintain one simple pipeline with stages: researched → contacted → owner conversation → walkthrough → technical fit → proposal → signed pilot → live → expanded/paused/declined. Log the next action and date on every active lead. Weekly review: visits, owner conversations, walkthroughs, proposals, major objections, and time spent per stage. Do not count a friendly staff conversation as an owner meeting.

For a live pilot, record a baseline from the venue's comparable days/times if available, then measure: booking-link clicks, attributable booked bay sessions **only if the venue shares them**, QR scans, started checkouts, completed payments, valid entries, support/refund issues, operator minutes per participant, and repeat attendance where consented and measurable. Use the venue's location/bay identifiers rather than an unattributed QR. Report small samples as counts, not as broad claims of revenue lift.

At day 30, ask the owner whether the experience helped the goal they named, whether staff burden was acceptable, and whether technical/support issues were resolved. Renew or expand only if both the owner and Pin2Win operations agree; otherwise pause, fix the obstacle, or decline the fit. Ask a successful venue for an introduction to **one** peer owner only with permission to reference the pilot's actual results.

## One-page leave-behind outline

Title: “A measurable skill-challenge add-on for your simulator bays.” Include: (1) what customers do, (2) what the venue keeps control of, (3) what Pin2Win supplies, (4) a three-step supervised-pilot concept, (5) “subject to technical, compliance, and written agreement approval,” and (6) direct contact details plus two meeting-time options. Replace generic “more revenue” language with the venue's own stated objective after discovery. Keep the first sheet prize-light and booking-forward.
