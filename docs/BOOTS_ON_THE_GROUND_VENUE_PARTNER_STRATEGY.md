# Pin2Win: boots-on-the-ground venue partner strategy

Field playbook for the partner leading local golf-simulator venue outreach. Updated September 16, 2026. San Antonio and nearby Boerne are the first territory because Pin2Win's current listed venue is Alamo Golf Den. Expand the radius only after the first repeatable venue pilot.

## The objective

Build straightforward, mutually beneficial partnerships with simulator venues. Pin2Win promotes the partner location and sends players to its booking channel. The venue keeps its bay-rental, membership, food-and-beverage, and other onsite revenue while allowing Pin2Win to operate its challenge through approved bays, QR signage, and a coordinated staff process. Pin2Win keeps its separate challenge-entry revenue. **There is no commission or revenue share between Pin2Win and the venue.**

The first ask is a **20-minute owner conversation and a no-payment walkthrough**, not a contract or public launch. The purpose is to confirm that the venue wants the marketing exposure, that the experience fits its customers, and that the challenge can operate reliably with little staff burden.

The acquisition target for the first 30 days is a working list of 20 qualified venues, 10 owner/GM conversations, 3 onsite walkthroughs, and 1–2 written pilot proposals. These are planning targets, not demand forecasts. Review the conversion rates weekly and adjust the territory or pitch before adding more visits.

## The win/win partnership

Pin2Win is an optional, separately paid golf skill challenge layered onto a venue's normal simulator booking. A customer discovers a partner through Pin2Win marketing, books bay time **directly with the venue**, scans an authorized QR code onsite, signs in or creates an account, pays Pin2Win through Square, receives challenge access, and plays under published rules.

The arrangement is intentionally easy to explain:

- **Pin2Win's win:** permission to offer and promote its challenge at an approved location, place approved QR signage, coordinate the simulator setup, and verify eligible results.
- **The venue's win:** ongoing exposure through Pin2Win's location directory and campaigns, direct traffic to its booking page, a new customer experience, and retention of 100% of its normal venue revenue.
- **The player's win:** a clear place to book and an additional skill-based experience during the simulator visit.
- **The clean boundary:** the venue keeps all revenue it collects; Pin2Win keeps all challenge-entry revenue it collects. Neither party receives a percentage of the other's sales.

Pin2Win handles its own account, challenge payment, rules, refunds, winner review, and prize administration. The venue controls its own pricing, bookings, memberships, admission, staff, facility, food and beverage, and customer service for venue purchases. The initial challenge entry fee currently published in the rules is $20 and is separate from bay rental. Never imply that the venue receives the entry fee or sponsors the prize.

Owner-facing value propositions to test, rather than claim as proven:

- An additional reason for customers to book a session or return with friends.
- A short event format for leagues, member nights, or corporate groups without replacing the venue's booking system.
- Pin2Win marketing that features the venue and sends interested players to the venue's own booking page.
- A location-specific listing, booking link, bay QR flow, and reporting that can show activity from that venue.
- Pin2Win-provided QR signage, promotional content, staff training, and issue escalation so the venue can participate without building a campaign or payment flow.

Lead with the owner's goals. For a membership club, ask about member engagement; for a social lounge, ask about group nights and quieter booking periods; for a coaching studio, ask whether a challenge fits its brand at all. A performance-only facility may be a poor fit, and that is useful to learn early.

## Build the target list before driving

Use Google Maps/Search and venue websites to find indoor golf venues; Google's [Business Profile guidance](https://business.google.com/us/business-profile/) explains why Maps and Search are useful for local business discovery. Cross-check simulator technology and bookings on the venue's own site or by calling the venue. The [Trackman locator](https://www.trackman.com/locator) is another discovery tool, **not** proof that Pin2Win can run on a particular bay.

For each prospect, record: venue name/address, owner or GM, direct contact method, bay count, simulator software and version, booking model, leagues/events, peak and slow periods, current challenge/tournament offers, suitability for an onsite QR, likely customer fit, and the source/date verified. Do not treat a lead as technically qualified until an operator confirms how an eligible shot and timestamp can be captured and reviewed.

### Priority score (0–10)

Score 0, 1, or 2 on each dimension: (1) owner access, (2) customer/event fit, (3) willingness to display approved QR signage and support a light operating process, (4) plausible simulator evidence path, and (5) quality of its booking page, imagery, and venue information for Pin2Win marketing. Visit 8–10 leads first, including different venue models; leave low-scoring leads for later. A high score earns a walkthrough, **not** permission to launch.

## The field sequence

1. **Prepare a one-page leave-behind and a phone demo.** Lead with the win/win exchange: “We market your location; you host the challenge; each business keeps its own revenue.” Show the venue listing, booking handoff, onsite QR-to-Square path, and an example admin report. Bring a proposed test-night outline and sample co-branded QR sign. Use a test account and sandbox/demo checkout; never take a live customer payment to demonstrate an unapproved venue.
2. **Make a short introduction.** Call or email to learn when the owner/GM is present. Walk in at a quiet time, buy a normal session or ask for permission to see a bay, and avoid interrupting staff or customers. If the owner is absent, leave a one-page note and ask for a specific callback time.
3. **Run a discovery conversation.** Ask about bookings, repeat visits, leagues, current promos, bay software, staffing, and what would make a pilot worth the effort. Spend more time listening than presenting.
4. **Offer an onsite walkthrough.** With the operator, trace one player from booking to QR scan to payment to simulator setup to recorded result and support escalation. Capture missing steps. If verification is not possible, propose only a technical dry run, not a paid pilot.
5. **Send a tailored pilot proposal within 24 hours.** State what the venue said it wants, the chosen bay/time, operator duties, QR placement, the Pin2Win marketing package, success measures, support path, and remaining prerequisites. State plainly that there is no revenue share. Ask for a yes/no decision date.
6. **Follow up once or twice, then close the loop.** Suggested cadence: next-day recap, day-4 question, day-10 final check. If there is no interest, record the objection and request permission to reconnect after a relevant product milestone. Do not repeatedly drop in uninvited.

### 30-second opener

> “Hi, I'm [name] with Pin2Win here in San Antonio. We partner with simulator venues on optional, QR-based golf skill challenges. The model is simple: Pin2Win markets your location and sends players to your booking page; you let us run the challenge with approved QR signage; and each business keeps its own revenue. Could I ask how you currently drive bookings and see whether a small test would fit here?”

### Owner discovery questions

- What are you trying to improve this quarter: new bay bookings, repeat visits, member engagement, or group events?
- Which days or times would you actually want to fill? Which times must a test avoid?
- Do you currently run closest-to-pin, hole-in-one, league, or corporate events? What works and what creates work for staff?
- What simulator hardware/software/version is in each candidate bay? Can it export a session, shot ID, exact shot time, holed flag, and an audit trail?
- Who would set up and supervise a challenge? What is a realistic training and support burden?
- How do guests book, and may Pin2Win link directly to that page from its location listings and campaigns?
- Which venue name, logo, photos, offers, and approved claims may Pin2Win use when marketing this location?
- Where can approved QR signs be placed, who approves them, and who checks that they remain visible and undamaged?
- Would the venue optionally repost Pin2Win campaign material or mention the challenge to existing customers? This is helpful but is not a substitute for Pin2Win's marketing responsibility.
- What would have to be true after a 30-day pilot for you to keep it?

### Common objections

| Objection | Response |
| --- | --- |
| “We already run tournaments.” | “Good. This should complement, not replace, yours. Which part of your current event has the most friction?” |
| “Staff cannot manage another system.” | “Let's map the exact operator steps first. If it takes too much staff time, we will not propose a paid pilot.” |
| “How do we get paid?” | “You continue collecting 100% of your normal booking, membership, food-and-beverage, and onsite revenue. Pin2Win separately collects its challenge fee. There is no revenue share in either direction.” |
| “What marketing do we receive?” | “Pin2Win creates and maintains your location listing and booking link, announces the partnership, and runs location-focused content and campaigns using assets you approve. The pilot proposal will name the exact deliverables and schedule so the commitment is measurable.” |
| “Will this cannibalize our booking revenue?” | “No challenge entry replaces the bay booking. Players book and pay you directly before using the onsite challenge. We will measure actual booking traffic rather than promise an uplift.” |
| “Does it work on our simulator?” | “We cannot promise that from the brand name. We need a bay-level setup and result-evidence walkthrough.” |
| “Who handles a disputed payment or winner?” | “Pin2Win handles its payment, entry, refund, and prize review. The pilot agreement will spell out what venue records or operator observations may be needed.” |

## The Pin2Win marketing commitment

Every active partner should receive a documented marketing package. Scale the frequency to Pin2Win's real capacity, but never tell a venue “we will market you” without naming the deliverables. The standard pilot package should include:

- A Pin2Win partner-location page with venue description, address, approved photos/logo, hours or booking guidance, challenge availability, and a direct venue booking link.
- Location and bay tracking so Pin2Win can report booking-link clicks, QR scans, completed entries, and other activity it can accurately attribute.
- A partnership-launch announcement on Pin2Win-owned social channels and inclusion in relevant “where to play” content.
- A reusable co-branded asset pack for social posts, email, lobby displays, and front-desk reference. Venue reposting is encouraged but optional unless agreed otherwise.
- Location-focused campaign content tied to appropriate moments such as launch week, league nights, corporate events, bad-weather play, or challenge milestones.
- Accurate referral links and calls to action that send customers to the venue's own booking system, not a competing reservation flow.
- A monthly partner recap covering work delivered, attributable traffic and entries, player feedback, and the next campaign opportunity.

Pin2Win must get written approval before using a venue's marks, photos, offers, or customer quotes. Marketing must not promise a specific number of bookings, guaranteed revenue, guaranteed prizes, numerical winning odds, or an affiliation with a simulator vendor that does not exist. Paid advertising budgets, if any, require separate written approval; the partnership itself does not create a revenue share.

## Pilot offer and partnership boundaries

Propose a **non-exclusive, one-venue/one-bay, 30-day pilot** only after technical and compliance readiness. Use this standard value exchange:

- Venue retains 100% of the bay-rental, membership, food-and-beverage, event, and other revenue it collects. It keeps control of pricing, reservations, admission, and customer service for those purchases.
- Pin2Win retains 100% of the separate challenge-entry revenue it collects and keeps control of challenge pricing, payment, rules, refunds, winner review, and prize administration.
- **No commissions and no revenue share:** neither party owes the other a percentage of sales under the partnership model. Do not negotiate or quote a percentage during outreach.
- Pin2Win supplies the agreed marketing package, approved QR signage, booking-link placement, landing-page copy, a staff quick-reference card, test accounts, activity reporting, and a named support contact.
- Venue supplies permission to run the challenge at an approved bay/time, permission to place approved QR signage, accurate booking/brand information, a staff contact, reasonable simulator/operator coordination, and agreed result/incident records. No extra hardware or vendor integration is promised.
- Venue approves use of its name, logo, imagery, and signage before publication. Each party owns its own customer list; Pin2Win does not receive or market to the venue's customer contacts without a separate lawful permission path.
- Any optional paid advertising, custom event, equipment, exclusivity, or sponsorship is outside the standard partnership and must be separately documented. These additions do not change the no-revenue-share model.
- Both parties get a written pause/stop procedure for payment outages, missing result evidence, unsafe setup, or disputes.

The most credible first pilot is a supervised event at an off-peak time. A 24/7 or unattended venue may require a different onsite and evidence control; do not force the same model onto it.

### Launch gate: do not skip this

The [platform compliance checklist](PLATFORM_COMPLIANCE_IMPLEMENTATION_CHECKLIST.md) still identifies transaction-specific onsite verification and real simulator-result integration as open. Before a **new paid venue launch**, Pin2Win operations must sign off on: current Challenge Details and published rules for that venue/bay; permitted Texas/18+ audience; prize funding/insurance and any venue-specific requirements; a reliable onsite-presence control; a tested Square payment-and-return flow; an authenticated simulator evidence path and winner review; refunds/support/reconciliation; and a written venue agreement. If these are not ready, continue acquisition conversations and technical dry runs only. Never make “guaranteed winner,” numerical odds, vendor endorsement, or “live at your venue” claims prematurely.

## Measure the work and decide what to do next

Maintain one simple pipeline with stages: researched → contacted → owner conversation → walkthrough → technical fit → proposal → signed pilot → live → expanded/paused/declined. Log the next action and date on every active lead. Weekly review: visits, owner conversations, walkthroughs, proposals, major objections, and time spent per stage. Do not count a friendly staff conversation as an owner meeting.

For a live pilot, record a baseline from the venue's comparable days/times if available, then measure both sides of the exchange: partner-page views, campaign reach, booking-link clicks, attributable booked bay sessions **only if the venue shares them**, QR scans, started checkouts, completed payments, valid entries, support/refund issues, operator minutes per participant, and repeat attendance where consented and measurable. Use the venue's location/bay identifiers rather than an unattributed QR. Report small samples as counts, not as broad claims of revenue lift.

At day 30, ask the owner whether the experience helped the goal they named, whether staff burden was acceptable, and whether technical/support issues were resolved. Renew or expand only if both the owner and Pin2Win operations agree; otherwise pause, fix the obstacle, or decline the fit. Ask a successful venue for an introduction to **one** peer owner only with permission to reference the pilot's actual results.

## One-page leave-behind outline

Title: “We market your location. You host the challenge. Each business keeps its revenue.” Include: (1) the two-sided value exchange, (2) the customer journey, (3) the specific Pin2Win marketing package, (4) what the venue authorizes and supplies, (5) the no-commission/no-revenue-share model, (6) a three-step supervised pilot, (7) “subject to technical, compliance, and written agreement approval,” and (8) direct contact details plus two meeting-time options. Replace generic “more revenue” language with the venue's stated objective and describe marketing deliverables precisely.
